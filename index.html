01 — PACIFIC IS CHANGING

Thermal expansion and shifting sea surface patterns set the baseline for Pacific island vulnerability.

Ocean waters absorb over 90% of the excess heat trapped in the Earth system.

When our oceans warm, the impacts reach far beyond the water's edge. Rising sea temperatures and thermal expansion create a baseline of vulnerability for Pacific island nations, fundamentally altering their surroundings.

Fig 1.1 — Ocean thermal indicators and surface anomaly distributions across the Pacific basin.

A changing environment is only part of the story.

Cyclones occur within this environmental backdrop — but exposure is not distributed evenly across populations.

02 — THE HAZARD

Trajectory, and frequency: mapping cyclone pathways across the basin.

Tropical cyclones are complex atmospheric phenomena fueled by ocean heat, atmospheric pressure gradients, and regional wind shear.

As ocean conditions shift, they fuel stronger and more frequent tropical cyclones across the basin. These storms do not strike randomly—their paths, intensity, and frequency reshape the daily realities for coastal populations.

Fig 2 — Cyclones, and density distribution across Pacific island territories.

However, looking at storm frequency alone still doesn't reveal the full picture.

The true impact lies in how geographic exposure translates directly into human and economic toll. •

03 — THE IMPACTS

Disproportionate losses: how geographic exposure translates into human and economic cost.

When a cyclone impacts an island territory, losses extend far beyond immediate physical damage. Geographic isolation, coastal infrastructure concentration, and limited capital reserves compound the social and economic disruption.

Smaller island economies and isolated communities often bear a disproportionately heavy share of the damage, where a single major event can wipe out years of economic growth and endanger countless lives.

Fig 3.1 — Impact on Population, and economic loss across affected island communities.

Ultimately, these interconnected environmental shifts demonstrate that climate impacts are not felt equally. Moving forward, understanding these complex dynamics is essential for creating targeted, evidence-based solutions.

The communities facing the highest risk are often those least responsible for global climate trends, yet they bear the heaviest burdens.

THE TAKEAWAY

By investing in resilient infrastructure and equitable support systems, we can help protect vulnerable coastal regions and build a safer, more sustainable future for everyone.
